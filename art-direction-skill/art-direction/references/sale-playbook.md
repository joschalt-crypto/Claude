# Sale / Black Friday playbook

Source: @aluncreative carousel "The sale is the story, not the sticker" (4 Oct 2026).
Core idea: the brands that win a sale don't post a red "40% off" banner on a white packshot.
They post a story. The price comes second, usually in the caption.

## The four concepts

### 1. The street poster
Print the sale like a lost-dog poster taped to a lamp post: the product, the date, and
tear-off tabs at the bottom (one tab per sale day). The pole, the tape and the street all
come from the prompt.
- Trick: draw the product half as a real photo, half as a pencil sketch, so it reads as "illustration".

### 2. Too much to carry
Show excess instead of a discount: a car boot full of branded bags, a stack of boxes taller
than the person carrying it, one bag so big it hides her. The logo is the only text.
The offer goes in the caption; the excess is the post.

### 3. The sign that says tomorrow
The day before the drop, post one sheet with almost nothing on it: a sketch clipped to a
hanger, a date sheet in a half-open filing drawer. People come back the next day to see what it was.

### 4. The paperwork
Sales are printed, typed and posted: a printer on the pavement spitting out "save the date"
sheets, a typewriter page that says "we are on sale", a newspaper pushed through a letterbox
("The Black Friday Times: out now"). Office props, expensive result.

## Carousel format (how she teaches it)
- Slide 1: hook. Slide 2: the thesis plus a stat ("11.8 billion dollars in one day. The brands
  that got their piece did not post a red banner.")
- Slides 3–6: one concept per slide: a numbered handwritten-style title, 2–3 plain sentences,
  then a grid of 2–3 example images. Optionally show "the product" next to the result.
- Light warm-grey background, thin black frames around the images.
- Call to action: comment a keyword (e.g. "Twist") to get the guide by DM.

## Adapting for Studio Yacy clients
- Small Basel shops: swap luxury for local. A poster on a Basel tram stop, a café's paper
  bags piled in a cargo bike, a sketch of tomorrow's cake on a hanger, a receipt-printer
  "save the date".
- **Studio Yacy voice: no fake urgency.** Avoid lines like "Only one week, then they're gone."
  Use honest dates and calm lines ("From Thursday. While it lasts." / "Ab Donnerstag.").
- Plan it in October and post in November (her pitch: ads cost more in November, so build early).
